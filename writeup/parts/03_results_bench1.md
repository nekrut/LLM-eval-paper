### Plan detail, not model identity, sets the ceiling

We ran the full plan gradient on the Jetson AGX Orin: 12 local open-weight executors x 7 plan conditions (Track B, v0.5, v1, v1g, v1.25, v1.5, v2) x 3 seeds, reasoning disabled, scored as per-sample orchestration success (Methods, *Scoring*). Of 324 cells, 323 completed in 7.8 h; one was classified as a generation failure rather than a capability score. A frontier reference gradient (claude-opus-5, claude-sonnet-5, claude-haiku-4-5) ran the same 7 conditions at 81/81 cells, no failures and no refusals, for $5.36 (Fig. 2). Neither gradient existed in the submitted version. <!-- addresses: R3.10, T3.6 -->

One interpretive limit governs everything that follows, and we state it here rather than in *Limitations*. At the detailed end of the gradient the plan contains the literal command line the script must emit; at the lean end it does not. A model that succeeds at v2 may be orchestrating a workflow or may be transcribing near-executable syntax, and this experiment cannot distinguish the two. The one internal comparison bearing on it is suggestive at best: v1g carries the same `lofreq call-parallel` invocation as v1.25 but extracted mechanically from the Galaxy IUC registry rather than authored by hand, and it does not reproduce the v1.25 lift for most local executors. That is one comparison between two plan files, not a comparison of planners. Benchmark 2 was designed partly because this question cannot be settled here. <!-- addresses: R3.preamble, C2, R2.4 -->

The gradient has a cliff. Without a plan, and with tool order alone (v0.5), every local executor scores at or near 0; at n=3, 11 of 12 reach 1.00 at v2. The frontier reference behaves differently at the lean end — 0.63 to 1.00 at Track B, where the local models score 0 — and saturates earlier, reaching 1.00 at v1g, after which v1.25 and v1.5 buy it nothing and claude-haiku-4-5 is indistinguishable from claude-opus-5. Model identity therefore does matter, and it matters most exactly where the plan is thinnest; the difference closes as plan detail rises. <!-- addresses: R2.4, C2 -->

The n=3 headline does not survive replication. We added 7 seeds to the original 3 at v2 Track A and pooled them (Fig. 3, Table 5). Nine of 12 executors are 10/10 (exact 95% CI [0.69, 1.00]); gpt-oss:20b is 9/10 ([0.55, 1.00]); qwen3.5:4b, which scored 3/3 at n=3, is 7/10 ([0.35, 0.93]); nemotron-3-nano:4b is 1/10 ([0.00, 0.45]). Pooled, 107 of 120 runs were perfect, 95% CI [0.82, 0.94]. Every model that moved, moved down, which is the expected direction rather than symmetric noise: a model that succeeds 70% of the time reaches 3/3 about a third of the time, while a model already at 1.00 cannot rise. A perfect 3/3 cell carries an exact 95% interval of [0.29, 1.00] (Wilson [0.44, 1.00]), and even 10/10 excludes only true rates below about 0.69. We therefore report the n=10 figures as the headline and treat every n=3 cell as an interval, not a point. <!-- addresses: R3.8, T3.6, R3.10 -->

A generational control makes the same point from the model side. qwen3.8:27b, released after the plans and scoring pipeline were fixed, pairs exactly with qwen3.6:27b on parameter count, quantization and inference engine. Mean orchestration success across the gradient is 0.57 and 0.67 respectively, and the two single-column differences are opposite in sign at n=3, so they read as sampling variation. Both fail without a plan and both need roughly v1g-level detail before they produce a working script: a newer, higher-benchmarking model did not move the cliff. <!-- addresses: R3.8, R2.4 -->

### The accuracy metric is degenerate by construction

Two reviewers asked for conventional variant-calling metrics in place of the tolerant Jaccard score. Over all 324 reasoning-disabled runs, TP = 1,042, FP = 0 and FN = 1,874, giving precision 1.000 (exact 95% CI [0.996, 1.000]), recall 0.357 ([0.340, 0.375]) and F1 0.527. Allele-frequency error on true positives is 0.0000, and 0 of 324 runs produced any allele frequency outside the +/-0.02 window. The accounting closes exactly: truth is 9 variants per run across the four samples, and 324 x 9 = 2,916 = TP + FN. For sampled successful runs the variant output is byte-identical to the answer key by md5 over CHROM/POS/REF/ALT/AF. Per-model and per-plan values are in Supplement Table S3. <!-- addresses: R3.4, C3, R2.11, R2.8 -->

These numbers carry no information the Jaccard score did not. LoFreq is deterministic: if the executor wires the pipeline correctly the output is exactly the canonical result, and if it does not, there is no output at all. The per-sample outcome is binary, so every accuracy metric layered on top collapses to one underlying quantity — did the model wire the pipeline correctly. Precision 1.000 and an allele-frequency error of exactly zero are properties of the task, not evidence of luck or of a well-chosen statistic; by the same argument the Jaccard score is degenerate by construction rather than by bad choice of measure. This is also the sharpest available answer to what the model contributes: the variant caller does the science, the executor wires it up, and orchestration is what is being measured. <!-- addresses: R2.11, R3.4, R3.2, C3 -->

The consequence is a limitation, not a defence. Making conventional accuracy metrics informative requires a task whose accuracy varies continuously — spike-ins across a range of allele frequencies, variable depth, a caller with tunable sensitivity. No such perturbation series was run. This is the strongest remaining argument for a third benchmark, and we state it as a gap rather than resolve it here. <!-- addresses: R3.3, C3 -->

### Enabling reasoning does not substitute for plan detail

A plan and a chain of thought both supply intermediate steps, so it is reasonable to expect them to substitute. We ran both arms of the gradient: 324 reasoning-disabled cells (1 failure, 0.3%, 7.8 h) and 270 reasoning-enabled cells (18 failures, 6.7%, 29.1 h), with zero CUDA faults across 37 h. The comparison is restricted to the 10 models runnable in both arms — both Granite models are excluded, because Ollama rejects the `think` parameter outright (HTTP 400) — and truncated generations are scored 0 rather than dropped. <!-- addresses: R3.16 -->

Reasoning helps where the plan is thin and costs accuracy where it is sufficient (Fig. 4). Per-plan deltas (enabled minus disabled) are +0.02 at Track B, +0.07 at v0.5, +0.18 at v1, +0.05 at v1g, +0.23 at v1.25, +0.07 at v1.5 and -0.20 at v2; including qwen3.8:27b deepens the v2 term to -0.27. At v2 Track A, 4 of 10 models regress, none improve and 6 are unchanged. One caveat travels with the result: the reasoning-enabled arm carries a 6.7% harness-bounded failure rate against 0.3% for the disabled arm, so it measures model-plus-budget rather than model capability alone. The v2 regression is not a budget artifact — those cells completed and were scored. <!-- addresses: R2.6, R3.16 -->

We took the model that failed hardest through a budget curve. For qwen3.8:27b on plan v1g, Track A, reasoning enabled, 3 seeds per budget: at 16k tokens, 3/3 truncated, 0/3 correct, ~2,200 s per cell; at 32k, 3/3 truncated, 0/3 correct, ~5,480 s; at 64k, 0/3 truncated, 1/3 correct, 7,395-12,974 s. The same condition with reasoning disabled at 16k is 3/3 correct in ~130 s. At 64k the model self-terminates after 35,000-55,000 tokens of reasoning, two to three times the budget the whole study uses, so the 16k and 32k rows are pure budget artifacts and carry no capability information. The defensible statement is narrow: enabling reasoning on this model requires a token budget two to three times larger merely to produce output, and even when that budget is supplied it reduces accuracy from 3/3 to 1/3 while increasing wall-clock cost 57- to 100-fold. <!-- addresses: R3.16, R3.8 -->

Two claims made earlier in this work do not survive, and we print them as retractions. First, that reasoning-enabled scripts are systematically more elaborate and that the elaboration is what breaks them: the median enabled/disabled script-size ratio at v2 across the other ten models is 1.00x, and the most elaborate script in the ablation (2,984 bytes, 8 staleness helpers) is the one that scored 1.00. The reasoning-enabled failures at v2 are ordinary bad bash — unterminated blocks, malformed pipes, a hallucinated `bwa` subcommand. Second, that the budget explanation was dead: moving from 16k to 64k moved the condition from 0/3 to 1/3, so the budget was genuinely part of the problem. <!-- addresses: R3.8 -->

An independent probe points the same way. gpt-oss:20b exposes a reasoning-effort control; holding prompt, seed, plan and hardware fixed at the model's native context, mean reasoning length spans 480, 10,053 and 139,705 characters across low, medium and high effort, a 290-fold manipulation. A sufficient plan (v2) is 3/3 at every effort level, an insufficient plan (v1) is 0/3 at every effort level, and only the intermediate band moves, non-monotonically, with medium (2/3 at v1.25) beating both ends. The same experiment limits our own generalisation: at a generous budget gpt-oss:20b is 3/3 at v2 at every effort, so the v2 reasoning penalty is not robust for that model and the general claim is narrowed to qwen3.8:27b specifically. A roughly 12-cell re-run at generous budget would settle whether any v2 penalty survives for the others; it was not run. Finally, `think: false` is a request rather than a guarantee: honoured by most models, rejected outright by both Granite models, and ignored by gpt-oss:20b, which emitted 47,606 and 60,523 characters of reasoning with the flag off. <!-- addresses: R3.16, R2.6 -->

### Injected failures: recovery is 12% where something actually breaks

Re-reading the PATH shims showed that a third of the injected error matrix injects no error: `slow_tool` sleeps 30 s and `stderr_warning_storm` emits 200 warning lines, and both then delegate to the real binary and succeed. They test latency tolerance and log-noise tolerance, which are legitimate robustness checks, but they are not recovery tests and must not be pooled with the five patterns that make a tool fail (Methods, Table 3). <!-- addresses: R3.7 -->

Splitting the local executor (qwen3.6:27b) on the baseline v2 plan along that line changes the reading of the result. On the 24 true-error cells the modal handle categories are crash 15, propagate 6 and recover 3: a recovery rate of 12% (exact 95% CI [0.03, 0.32]) against a crash rate of 62% ([0.41, 0.81]). On the 12 non-error cells the script recovers 12 times, trivially, because the tool succeeded, as it does on the 3 control cells. Pooled across all 36 cells this reads as 42% ([0.26, 0.59]), the framing used in the submitted manuscript, which we withdraw. The frontier executor (claude-opus-4-7) gives an identical distribution on the identical split — crash 15, propagate 6, recover 3, 12% — so the cell-for-cell match between a free local model and a frontier API model is real, and holds on the harder subset. <!-- addresses: R3.7, R2.7, C1 -->

Nothing here shows that either executor handles injected failures well: on cells where something actually went wrong, both crash 62% of the time. What changes the outcome is the plan, not the model. Under the v2_defensive plan, which adds a `try()` helper, per-step output validation, retry-once and a structured failure log, crash and propagate disappear across all 36 cells for both executors, every cell resolves to recover (21) or partial (15), and the mean variant-overlap score rises from 0.333 to 0.625 for the local and the frontier executor alike. This is the plan-sufficiency result arriving from the error side. [[NEEDS-NUMBER: v2_defensive handle-category counts split by true-error vs non-error cells; the defensive rows are reported pooled only]] <!-- addresses: R3.7, R2.7 -->

These runs had no opportunity to retry: the script was generated once, executed once and scored, so when a tool failed there was no observation step and no second attempt. A 12% recovery rate under a single pass is the strongest internal motivation for the repair loop both reviewers asked for, and the honest reading of benchmark 1 is that it measures what a model does without a loop. Benchmark 2 supplies the loop. <!-- addresses: C1, R2.10, R3.7 -->
